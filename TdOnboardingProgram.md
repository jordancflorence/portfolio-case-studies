# 📋 Credit Fulfillment Onboarding Program

**Role:** Operations Officer III, TD Business Banking Credit Fulfillment
**Timeline:** 2024
**Format:** Self-initiated program design; adopted by team leadership

---

## 🏦 Context

TD Business Banking's credit fulfillment operations team processes funding
for business credit deals under a strict 24-hour SLA. The work ranges from
routine credit transactions to complex multi-party deals worth up to
$750,000 - including business acquisitions, real estate closings, and
government-backed funding arrangements. The stakes are high, the pace is
relentless, and the compliance requirements are non-negotiable.

When I joined the team as a new Operations Officer, no formal onboarding
program existed. New officers learned by shadowing tenured colleagues. Full
system access took six to eight weeks to obtain. The procedure library was
incomplete and frequently out of sync with actual compliance requirements.
Personal note-taking was treated as a mandatory survival skill in place of
reliable institutional documentation.

Within my first week, my team manager of over twenty years announced her
departure. Her superior left shortly after. The team was left without
leadership during one of the most demanding onboarding periods I have
experienced in my career.

---

## ⚠️ The Problem

The gap between what a new officer needed to know and what the existing
onboarding process provided was significant - and the consequences were
not abstract.

Three compounding problems defined the existing approach:

- 🔁 **Knowledge transfer** depended entirely on the availability of
  tenured officers, creating bottlenecks and inconsistency in what new
  hires actually learned
- 📄 **The procedure library** did not reflect current compliance
  requirements, forcing officers to rely on outdated documentation or
  informal personal workarounds
- 📊 **No structured progression** existed from supervised to independent
  work - no quality checkpoints, no milestones, no way to confirm an
  officer was genuinely ready to handle complex deals on their own

I experienced all of this firsthand. I decided something should be done
about it.

---

## 🔧 The Approach

This program was not assigned to me. It was not part of my job description.

**Requirements gathering** came first. I conducted informal interviews with
tenured officers to understand their workflows, the pain points they
encountered consistently, and the gaps between the procedure library and
actual practice. I facilitated pain point sessions with the broader team
during the three-month period when leadership was absent - creating a
structured space for officers to surface concerns and shape what a better
onboarding experience could look like.

Because I was still new to the role myself, I deliberately sought out
expertise I did not have. I worked closely with an internal Business Analyst
who had previously held my role and had since transitioned into a strategic
capacity. He helped me identify the quality metrics a compliance-driven
environment required: the specific criteria that would determine whether an
officer was genuinely ready to work independently, not just whether enough
time had passed.

**The program I built included:**

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| 📅 Structured onboarding schedule | Learning sequenced deliberately across the first weeks in role |
| ✅ Knowledge retention checkpoints | Embedded throughout the schedule, not bolted on at the end |
| 📈 Graduated progression model | Officers advanced based on demonstrated competency, not time elapsed |
| 📁 Knowledge transfer documentation | Onboarding no longer dependent on any single person's availability |
| 🏗️ Complex deal coverage | Standard credit transactions and high-stakes multi-party arrangements |

The goal was not to cover everything. In a role with this many variables,
comprehensive documentation is neither achievable nor sufficient. The goal
was to ensure new officers were structurally set up to handle the pace --
and that the program could withstand personnel changes without degrading.

---

## 📈 The Outcome

When the incoming team manager arrived three months into the process, the
program was reviewed, adopted, and handed off to an internal Business
Analyst for ongoing stewardship.

Feedback received around the time of my departure indicated that the
incoming manager had actioned the core criteria I had proposed, and that
the structural gaps I had experienced as a new officer were being addressed
for those who came after.

> The program was built by someone who had just lived the problem it was
> designed to solve. That proximity was both the motivation and the
> methodology.

---

## 💡 What This Demonstrates

This project reflects what I do when I encounter a broken process and have
the capacity to address it: identify the gap, gather requirements from the
people closest to the work, collaborate with subject matter experts to fill
what I don't know, and build something structured enough to outlast my own
involvement.

| Skill | Application |
|---|---|
| Stakeholder interviews | Tenured officers and internal BA |
| Requirements gathering | Pain point sessions, workflow mapping |
| Quality metric design | Competency criteria for independent readiness |
| Documentation | Onboarding schedule, progression framework |
| Cross-functional collaboration | Partnered with internal Business Analyst |

The context was a high-stakes regulated banking environment. The output was
a program that was adopted and handed off without me having to campaign for
it further.
