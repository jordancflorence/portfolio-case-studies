# 💳 Bill Payment Workflow Redesign

**Role:** Personal Banking Associate, TD Canada Trust
**Timeline:** 2023
**Format:** Internal ideation submission with supporting UI mockup

---

## 🏦 Context

TD Canada Trust's EasyWeb online banking platform underwent a significant UI
refresh. The update modernized the interface but left a gap in one of the most
common client workflows: paying multiple bills in a single session.

As the branch's go-to resource for technology questions, I regularly fielded
client feedback about their digital banking experience - not through formal
channels, but through direct conversation at my desk. Clients trusted me to
listen and, where I could, to help.

---

## ⚠️ The Problem

After the UI refresh, clients making multiple bill payments had to complete
each one as a fully separate transaction. Pay one bill, return to the bill
payment home screen, start again from scratch. Repeat for every payee.

This friction came up repeatedly in client conversations - unprompted, across
different clients, over an extended period. No batch workflow existed. No way
to queue multiple payments and execute them together. Each additional bill
meant restarting the entire process.

> It was a gap that was easy to miss from an engineering perspective and
> impossible to miss from the front line.

---

## 🔧 The Approach

I decided to do something with what I was hearing rather than log it as a
complaint and move on.

Working within the constraints of a retail branch environment - limited
software access due to TD's compliance requirements - I used Canva during
downtime to build a UI mockup of a proposed solution.

**The concept:**

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| 📋 Single payment screen | One interface to capture all payees in a session |
| ➕ Add payee rows | Each row captures a payee and payment amount |
| 💰 Batch execution | All payments submitted together in one action |
| ✅ Funds validation | Total checked against available balance before execution |

I then wrote a structured submission for TD's internal ideation portal -
a platform accessible to all retail banking staff across the organization.

**The submission included:**

- 🔍 A problem statement grounded in observed client friction
- 📊 A current-state description of the broken workflow
- 🔄 A future-state proposal outlining the multi-payment experience
- 🛠️ A brief implementation note for the technical team reviewing it

The submission was written to be as clear and professional as the platform
itself - knowing it could be read by anyone from a branch manager to a
product team.

---

## 📈 The Outcome

The submission was picked up and merged with similar ideas submitted by other
associates across the bank - a signal that the friction I was observing was
not isolated to my branch. Shortly after submission, the portal flagged the
idea as having been moved toward implementation.

The confirmation that mattered most came from a client. Someone who had
originally described the frustration to me returned to the branch and said
directly: whatever you did, it worked. The multi-payment functionality had
been added to the EasyWeb platform.

> The final implementation wasn't a direct reproduction of my mockup - it
> never is, and I didn't expect it to be. But the structured framing of the
> problem and the clarity of the proposed solution were enough to move the
> idea forward without further advocacy on my part.
